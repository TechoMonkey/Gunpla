# High Grade starter chassis and builds

Prototype draft v0.1 · November 20, 2026

Choose a chassis, then equip weapons, a shield, and upgrades. The approved base stats and Move and Cool orders belong to the chassis. Equipment contributes its own orders, counterattacks, stat modifiers, and passive capabilities. Chassis and component points are initial playtest proposals. The example complete starter builds each target 100 points.

The Shielding values above are chassis base stats. The two starter shield components grant defensive orders and counterattacks. Their listed effects determine any additional Shielding during play.

Component cards and their complete order statistics are in the [starter equipment catalog](<C:/Work/Gunpla Wars/outputs/starter-equipment-catalog-v0.1.md>). The starter weapons, shields, and General upgrades are compatible with both starter chassis when slot capacity permits. Specialized upgrades must meet their printed chassis requirements. The HG Starter GM supports Flight upgrades; the HG Starter Zaku II is incompatible with Flight upgrades.

All upgrade types use the same three upgrade slots. The Flight Backpack uses one upgrade slot when its chassis compatibility requirement is met.

Each named weapon or upgrade is limited to one copy per Gunpla unless its card explicitly allows additional copies. Each allowed copy consumes its listed slots and points. Different Gunpla can each equip their own copy.

## Building a suit

1. Choose the Gunpla model, chassis grade, and version.
2. Select equipment within the chassis's 2 weapon slots, 1 shield slot, and 3 upgrade slots. Check each item's slot cost, compatibility requirements, and copy limit.
3. Apply equipped passive stat modifiers once to the chassis base values to obtain the assembled stats.
4. Combine the chassis orders with orders and counterattacks granted by equipped items. Display the source item on each granted order.
5. Apply relevant upgrade modifiers to those orders. Each attack order displays its resolved attack dice, damage per hit, SP, range, and hit requirements.
6. Add chassis points and all equipped-item points for the suit's total cost.
7. Reveal the complete build and its agreed physical representation scale before deployment.

During an activation, choose one available order from the assembled list. Temporary bonuses from resolved orders apply during play according to their printed durations.

Use the foundation rules for activation heat, cooling exemptions, restored orders, movement, Jump, combat, and objective control. Each suit can use its cooling order once per round.

Each copy independently tracks its Hull, heat, activation count, disabled orders, timed bonuses, and uses of every once-per-round order or counterattack.

## Shared orders

| Order | Phase | Weight | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Move | 1 | 15 | Move up to the current Movement stat, using the suit's current Jump capability under the foundation movement rules. Unmodified starters have Movement 6 inches and Jump 2. |
| Cool | 1 | 5 | Apply the suit's current cooling strength, restore all heat-disabled normal orders and counterattacks, and gain the heat-roll exemption for the remainder of the round. Usable once per round by that suit. |

Apply normal activation heat after either order finishes. Cooling's exemption applies to the subsequent heat check.

## Timing clarifications for this draft

- Spend an order's once-per-round use when it executes. A normal order canceled before execution still spends its team activation under the foundation rules; its order-specific use remains unspent.
- Cooling restores heat-disabled entries while preserving already-spent once-per-round uses.
- An Armor or Shielding bonus that has resolved lasts for its stated duration, even if heat later disables its source order. A defeated suit uses the standard wreck profile.
- The random heat-disable pool includes eligible normal orders and counterattacks that are not already heat-disabled, including once-per-round entries whose uses have been spent. Cool, passive capabilities, Jump, and Flight remain protected under the foundation rules.
- Track each equipment-granted order's availability separately. A weapon's other granted orders and its equipped passive effects continue to follow their own availability and duration rules.
- Shield Bash cancels an attack step within an order that has begun. Finish the remaining order and apply its normal activation heat. A whole normal order disabled by heat before execution follows the separate cancellation rule and generates zero activation heat.
- Recheck each declared counterattack before it executes. If Shield Bash resolves first, another counterattack requiring that same pending ranged attack is canceled because the shot has already been canceled. It spends zero uses and adds zero heat from its ability. All responses still come from the original action.

## Suggested first playtest

Use the standard 36-inch-square objective-control mission. The Zaku player fields Zaku A Gunner and Zaku B Heavy Gunner. The GM player fields GM A Assault and GM B Marksman. Each player fields 200 proposed points and receives two activations per round for the game.

Track each suit's cooling use, defensive-order use, and counterattack use independently. Track Shoulder Brace through the current turn and Shield Up through the current round.

Record damage, order choices, heat-check frequency, canceled counterattacks, and objective scores. Review the 100-point target costs after a complete match.

## Grades and representation scale

Gameplay grades progress through HG, RG, MG, and PG. Higher-grade chassis versions of the same model provide stronger base stats, additional chassis orders, or both. A complete build's points cost includes its chassis and all equipped items.

Players select each unit's chassis grade, version, and equipment while building their team and reveal the complete builds and physical representation scales before deployment. Mixed-grade teams use the normal shared points budget.

A replacement kit for the same Gunpla must match its gameplay grade's agreed physical representation scale. HG and RG kits at 1/144 can substitute for one another. PG profiles use 1/60 as their reference scale.

Both players may agree to a shared alternate scale for a gameplay grade. For example, both may use 1/144 HG kits with PG profiles, using PG stats, orders, and points costs. Apply the alternate representation scale to all units of that grade on both teams.
